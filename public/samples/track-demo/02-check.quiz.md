# Check: Planets, Stars and Motion

## Q1 · Why do stars twinkle while planets usually don't?

- [ ] Stars pulse in brightness; planets shine steadily
- [x] Stars are point sources, so atmospheric distortions aren't averaged out
- [ ] Planets are brighter, and bright objects can't twinkle
- [ ] Starlight has to cross more of the atmosphere than planet light

> A star arrives as a single point of light, so every small deflection by moving
> air shows up as a flicker. A planet arrives as a small disc, many rays at
> once, and the wobbles cancel out.

## Q2 · Which of these are consequences of the Earth's spin, rather than its orbit?

```quiz
type: multi
points: 2
```

- [x] Stars rise in the east and set in the west over a night
- [ ] Orion is a winter constellation in the northern hemisphere
- [x] The whole sky appears to rotate around the pole star
- [ ] Different constellations are visible in July and in January

> Nightly motion comes from the spin; seasonal changes in which constellations
> are visible come from the orbit.

## Q3 · Match each observation with the motion that explains it.

```quiz
type: match
points: 3
```

| Observation | Cause |
|---|---|
| A star sets four minutes earlier each night | Earth's orbit around the Sun |
| The Moon rises later each evening | The Moon's orbit around the Earth |
| Shadows are shortest around midday | Earth's spin on its axis |

## Q4 · The word "planet" comes from the Greek for ___.

**Answer:** wanderer

> Planets drift against the fixed background of the stars from week to week,
> which is exactly what earned them the name.
